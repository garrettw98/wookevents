# Camp Tahquitz, April 23–26, 2027: event plan

Experimental bass and dubstep campout. Three days of music at The Bowl, afters at the K Lodge and The Dome until sunrise, everyone off site Monday.

The numbers in this plan come from one budget model. Open [`budget.html`](budget.html) to change any input, including every ticket tier's price and size, and see profit, break-even and the split with Kinetic Desert update.

## The short version

1. **The full lineup needs 235 paid tickets to break even. Reeguez Rocks sold 90.** That is 2.6 times the last turnout before anyone makes a dollar. If the full plan sells 90 again, the loss is about $20,400, or $10,200 each for you and Kinetic Desert.
2. **Site costs alone are covered by about 80 tickets.** Everything above that is the lineup. Each $1,000 of artist cost adds five or six tickets to break-even, so the size of the lineup is the size of the bet.
3. **Let the alumni presale decide how big to book.** Run it December 1–15, before the headliner contracts are signed. A leaner lineup with $2,000 headliners breaks even at 188 tickets and loses $12,200 at 90. Upgrade to $3,000 headliners if the presale sells out.
4. **At a 290-ticket sell-out the full plan makes about $10,700**, or $5,350 each, plus Kinetic Desert's $2,000 guarantee.
5. **The 500 unlock is where the money is.** At 440 paid the model shows about $35,600. Now that the main stage can move to a larger area, the open items are what the second stage costs and where 500 people go after midnight.
6. **Staying under 199 on site does not work as a plan.** Only 139 tickets fit, and even the leaner lineup loses about $3,300 with all of them sold. It matters as a fallback: if sales stall, capping at 139 saves the $3,000 permit.
7. **The crew plan is short by more than half.** Twelve ticket-only volunteers close the gap.
8. **Stagecoach is the same weekend (April 23–25) in Indio.** Porto-potties, generators, rental cars and flights into Ontario and Palm Springs will be tight.

**The 500 unlock.** The main stage moves to a larger area, The Bowl becomes a side stage, and Kinetic Desert provides production for both. In the model the unlock adds about $4,200 and pays for itself after roughly 25 extra tickets. Two things are not in that number:

- **Programming a second stage.** Either the 24 sets are spread across two stages or more acts are booked. More acts means more fees.
- **Any change to Kinetic Desert's guarantee** for running a second stage.

Afters still hold 145 people indoors (105 in the K Lodge, 40 in The Dome). At 500 on site that needs a third space.

## Tickets

### Price ladder

Tiers sell in order. Quantities assume 290 paid tickets.

| Ticket | Presale | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Final | Average at sell-out |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fri–Sun | $169 × 50 | $189 × 60 | $209 × 50 | $229 × the rest | $195 |
| Sat–Sun | none | $139 × 20 | $155 × 30 | $169 × the rest | $155 |
| Sunday only | none | none | $79 × 15 | $85 × the rest | $82 |

- Early tickets are the cheap ones, so the average price is lower when fewer sell: about $155 at 90 tickets and $174 at 290. The model now counts this, which is one reason break-even moved up from my first estimate.
- The $40 gap between Fri–Sun and Sat–Sun is deliberately small. It makes Friday feel close to free, which pushes people to arrive Friday.
- Hold Sunday-only tickets until set times are out, about a month before.
- Each Fri–Sun ticket nets its price less the $30 venue fee. The Dome fee is capped at $2,000, so it stops costing anything after the 200th ticket.
- Add single-day Friday or Saturday tickets only if sales are behind in March.

**The 7:00 gap.** One-hour sets from 2:00 to 7:30 leave half an hour over. I put it at 7:00, when the sun is going down (sunset is around 7:25 PM that weekend). Use it to switch the stage to its night look and for one short shared moment each day. The alternative is to make 6:00–7:30 a single 90-minute sunset set.

**Saturday carries the most money, Sunday the least.** Every ticket type includes Sunday, so the Sunday headliner sells almost no extra tickets. Its job is to keep people on site. A one-time back-to-back from two acts already there from Friday or Saturday, at $750–$1,000 extra each, does that with no new flight.

**What each artist gets.** The fee, plus what you described: a room in the K Lodge apartment on the night they play one of the top three slots, a bed in a shared cabin if they stay longer, meals for their whole stay, and rides to and from Ontario or Palm Springs. Flights are the one piece still open (see [Questions](#questions-that-would-change-this-plan)).

**Placing the four names.** Ask each for a quote, then let the quote set the slot: $2,500 and up plays 10:30, $1,500–$2,000 plays 9:00, $750–$1,000 plays 7:30. If two quote similar numbers, a back-to-back at 10:30 puts both names on the poster for one headline slot.

**Guest list.** One guest for daytime artists and two for 7:30 and later comes to 33 guests, already inside the 60 unticketed people.

### Afters

Midnight to just past sunrise (about 6:05 AM), all three nights. No booked artists.

- **K Lodge, 105 people:** the loud room. Bowl artists, back-to-backs.
- **The Dome, 40 people:** deep, slow and strange. Lean into the plush interior.
- **A third space with no sound system:** fire pit or tea lounge near the K Lodge. With 145 indoor spots for up to 350 people, the overflow needs somewhere warm to sit.

Give each room a host each night and a sign-up board that opens at 10:00 PM, with 45-minute slots. Put a crew member on the K Lodge door with a counter.

**The midnight move** is the riskiest ten minutes of each night: up to 350 people leave an amphitheater on a mountainside in the dark and the cold at the same time. Light that path first. Start both afters rooms at 11:45 so there is music when people arrive.

**Keeping people Sunday night.** Announce the Sunday closer as a one-off, and make Monday brunch the meal you serve everyone if the budget allows (about $900 for 300 people). It also means fewer people drive down the mountain tired at 1:00 AM.

## Safety, permits and weather

- **Cold.** The camp sits around 6,000 feet or higher. Expect nights near freezing, and late-April snow is possible. You have October there for comparison; I did not find a reliable late-April average for the camp itself. Tell ticket buyers plainly what to pack.
- **Rain or snow at The Bowl.** There is no covered space for 350 people. The K Lodge holds 105. Decide the bad-weather plan and the refund terms before tickets go on sale.
- **Medical.** The nearest hospital is a long drive down Highway 38. Have at least one EMT or wilderness first responder on site each night, naloxone, a harm-reduction table, and a written plan for who calls whom and where an ambulance meets you.
- **Insurance.** Confirm the $3M/$1M policy names the camp, covers overnight camping, and says what it does about alcohol people bring.
- **Fire.** Ask the camp what the forest's fire rules allow in April for fire pits and fire performance.
- **Stagecoach.** Reserve porto-potties, generators, light towers and radios as soon as the 500 decision is made, or earlier on a refundable hold.

## Questions that would change this plan

1. **Who pays for flights?** I budgeted $2,000. If artists book their own, break-even drops from 235 to 223.
2. **What did a Reeguez Rocks ticket cost?** The 90 tells me the crowd size. The price tells me whether $169–$229 is a step up or in line.
3. **At 500, does the side stage need its own acts, and does Kinetic Desert's guarantee change?**
4. **When is the permit deadline, and is the $3,000 due at filing?** The fallback of capping at 139 only works if the decision can wait for sales data.
