# Camp Tahquitz, April 23–26, 2027: event plan

Experimental bass and dubstep campout. Three days of music at The Bowl, afters at the K Lodge and The Dome until sunrise, everyone off site Monday.

The numbers in this plan come from one budget model. Open [`budget.html`](budget.html) to change any input and see profit, break-even and the split with Kinetic Desert update.

## The short version

1. **As outlined, the event needs 249 of its 290 sellable tickets to break even.** That is 86% sold before anyone makes a dollar, with about $6,000 of profit at a full sell-out. The artist curve you described costs $18,000–$21,500, and at 350 people on site there are only about 290 paid tickets to carry it.
2. **Four changes bring break-even down to 210 tickets (72%) and sell-out profit up to about $13,500.** Price the Fri–Sun ticket to average $195 instead of $180, sell 7 of the 8 rented cabins, add about $10 a head of merch, meal-plan and vendor income, and spend less on Sunday's top slots than on Friday's and Saturday's.
3. **Plan for 350 on site and treat 500 as an unlock, decided by March 15.** Going to 500 adds roughly $4,200 of cost and pays for itself after about 25 extra tickets. At 440 paid the model shows about $34,000 of profit. The check to make first is whether The Bowl and the afters rooms can take that crowd (see [Capacity](#capacity)).
4. **The under-199 version does not work with this lineup.** With 60 crew, artists and guests on site, only 139 tickets fit under the permit line. Even with a cut-down $11,300 artist budget it clears about $1,200 at a sell-out.
5. **The crew plan is short by about half.** The gate math covers one person per shift and nothing else, and the 8 general crew supply 24 shifts against roughly 53 needed. Ten ticket-only volunteers close the gap.
6. **Stagecoach is the same weekend (April 23–25) in Indio.** The audience does not overlap, but porto-potties, generators, radios, rental cars and flights into Ontario and Palm Springs will be tight. Book those early.

## Capacity

Every limit is counted on people on site, so the 60 unticketed people come off the top.

| On-site limit | Paid tickets that fit | Added cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 199 | 139 | None | Not viable with $3,000 headliners |
| 350 | 290 | $3,000 permit | The plan |
| 500 | 440 | Permit plus porto-potties, more medical and site supplies | Unlock if sales support it |

**The permit step.** Crossing 199 on site costs about $3,150 with contingency, which is the net from roughly 19 tickets. Never end up between 140 and 160 paid tickets. Since break-even sits above 200 in every realistic version, file for the permit from the start and ask the county for its lead time now.

**The 500 unlock.** Decide by March 15. Unlock if about 230 tickets are sold by then and sales are still moving, because that pace reaches 290 before the event and the next 150 tickets are nearly all margin. Before promising it, check three things:

- The Bowl holds 350. At 500 on site, a headliner could draw more than that. Ask the camp whether the 350 figure is seating or a hard limit.
- Afters hold 145 people indoors (105 in the K Lodge, 40 in The Dome). At 350 on site that is already tight on a cold night. At 500 it needs a third space.
- Parking and car camping. I do not know the lot size.

### The Bowl, each day

| Time | Length | Fri | Sat | Sun |

Total: $18,325 across 24 sets.

**The 7:00 gap.** One-hour sets from 2:00 to 7:30 leave half an hour over. I put it at 7:00, when the sun is going down (sunset is around 7:25 PM that weekend). Use it to switch the stage to its night look and for one short shared moment each day: a welcome on Friday, a group photo on Saturday, thank-yous on Sunday. The alternative is to make 6:00–7:30 a single 90-minute sunset set.

**Saturday carries the most money, Sunday the least.** Every ticket type includes Sunday, so the Sunday headliner sells almost no extra tickets. Its job is to keep people on site. Two ways to do that for less than $3,000:

- Close Sunday with a one-time back-to-back from two acts who are already there from Friday or Saturday, paying each $750–$1,000 extra. No new travel, and it is a set people cannot see elsewhere.
- Put the one fresh Sunday name at 9:00 PM.

**Book in two waves.** The six top slots on Friday and Saturday plus Sunday's 9:00 PM act come to $12,500. Contract those first, since they sell the tickets. Hold the Sunday closer and the 15 daytime slots (about $5,800 together) until the alumni presale shows demand. Daytime slots suit Southern California artists who bring friends, and they can be booked late.

**Placing the four names.** Ask each for a quote that includes travel, then let the quote set the slot: $2,500 and up plays 10:30, $1,500–$2,000 plays 9:00, $750–$1,000 plays 7:30. If two of them quote similar numbers, a back-to-back at 10:30 gets both names on the poster for one headline fee each night.

**Guest list.** One guest for daytime artists and two for 7:30 and later comes to 33 guests. That is already inside the 60 unticketed people.

### Afters

Midnight to just past sunrise (about 6:05 AM), all three nights. No booked artists.

- **K Lodge, 105 people:** the loud room. Bowl artists, back-to-backs.
- **The Dome, 40 people:** deep, slow and strange. Lean into the plush interior.
- **A third space with no sound system:** fire pit or tea lounge near the K Lodge. With 145 indoor spots for up to 350 people, the overflow needs somewhere warm to sit that is not a tent.

Give each room a host each night and a sign-up board that opens at 10:00 PM, with 45-minute slots. Open decks without one tend to go to whoever is pushiest, or go quiet at 3:00 AM. Put a crew member on the K Lodge door with a counter.

**Keeping people Sunday night.** Announce the Sunday closer as a one-off, and make Monday brunch the meal you serve everyone if the budget allows (about $900 for 300 people). It also means fewer people drive down the mountain tired at 1:00 AM.

## Safety, permits and weather

- **Cold.** The camp sits around 6,000 feet or higher. Expect nights near freezing, and late-April snow is possible. I did not find a reliable late-April average for the camp itself, so check its own records. Tell ticket buyers plainly what to pack.
- **Rain or snow at The Bowl.** There is no covered space for 350 people. The K Lodge holds 105. Decide the bad-weather plan and the refund terms before tickets go on sale.
- **Medical.** The nearest hospital is a long drive down Highway 38. Have at least one EMT or wilderness first responder on site each night, naloxone, a harm-reduction table, and a written plan for who calls whom and where an ambulance meets you.
- **Insurance.** Confirm the $3M/$1M policy names the camp, covers overnight camping, and says what it does about alcohol people bring.
- **Fire.** Ask the camp what the forest's fire rules allow in April for fire pits and fire performance.
- **Permit.** Ask the county for its filing deadline this month.
- **Stagecoach.** Reserve porto-potties, generators, light towers and radios as soon as the 500 decision is made, or earlier on a refundable hold.

2. **How is "profit" defined with Kinetic Desert?** Is the $2,000 on top of their share or an advance against it, and who carries a loss?
3. **Is $250 per cabin for the whole event or per night?**
4. **How many car camping spots and parking spaces are there?**
5. **Is The Bowl's 350 a hard limit?** The 500 unlock depends on it.
