    Scale stages

    Each stage is a limit on people on site. Partners, crew and hard costs name the stage they start at, so the tickets that fit are whatever is left after everyone without a ticket. Stepping into a stage costs money before it earns any. Two things cover that. The step fund holds back a percent of everything above the floors at a stage, from every share alike, and pays for the step. If the step is never taken, the fund stays in your profit. The rest is covered by selling the larger stage as one block: buyers past a sell-out join a waitlist and are not charged until enough are waiting that the step leaves you no loss. Type a count under "Release at" to set that yourself, or leave 0. No artist and no budget is paid less after a step. "Worth it from" is where the event as a whole, and then your own take, are back above the sell-out of the stage before.

      Where the artists stand

        How it works

        • The minimums are owed whatever sells, and count toward break-even.
        • Past break-even, every open slot rises toward its full fee at the same pace, so they all get there together.
        • Daytime slots have no minimum. They are paid from break-even on.
        • An agreed slot is paid on its own terms. A rate of 0 is a flat fee, and what it saves goes to the other slots and then to profit.

        The booking queue

        The order to book and announce in. "Covered at" is the ticket count at which sales pay for the hard costs plus every minimum down to that row. The last row is the break-even for the whole lineup.
